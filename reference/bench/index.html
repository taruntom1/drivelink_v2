Reference build

Use this list when choosing parts for the PC bench or a later Pi robot. Start with the board and cable. A single motor and encoder are enough for the first spin. Add the second wheel, IMU, and Pi when you need them.

Part What to check
ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 Choose a variant with 16 MB flash, such as N16R8. Confirm your board has the pins in the wiring tables. The bench uses its USB-to-UART port and a USB data cable. Espressif board guide
Pololu G2 High-Power Motor Driver 18v17 Use one current-revision board (marked md31c) per brushed DC motor. Check the driver and connector ratings against the motor’s running and stall current. Include a supply capacitor as specified by Pololu. Pololu specifications and pinout
12 V JGB37-520-class gear motor with encoder Check the exact model’s rated and stall current, pinout, encoder supply and output levels, counts per motor turn, and gear ratio. Check the output shaft and mounting before choosing wheels.
Fused 12 V motor supply Use a current-limited supply. Choose the fuse and wire size for your actual parts. Follow the driver vendor’s guidance for supply voltage spikes and motor braking.
Adafruit BNO055 breakout Optional for the bench. Use the breakout's I²C connections and mount it so you know which way its axes point. Adafruit pinouts
Raspberry Pi 5 and buck converter The Pi is for the later robot. A buck converter steps the 12 V rail down for compatible loads. For the Pi, use its recommended USB-C power supply on the bench or a vehicle power solution that meets its USB-C requirements. Raspberry Pi power supply requirements
Bench hardware Secure the motor or chassis, support the wheels clear of the bench, and put the motor power switch within reach. Use motor and power wiring rated for the current.

What else works

  • A substitute driver needs PWM + DIR inputs for sign-magnitude control at 3.3 V logic and must support the motor’s voltage and current. It must accept the PWM frequency: 25 kHz, unless you change pwm_freq_hz of its actuator (5 to 100 kHz). For example, Cytron lists a 20 kHz maximum for the MDD10A. Cytron specifications
  • Use an encoder with two quadrature channels. Keep ESP inputs at 3.3 V logic. If the encoder outputs 5 V, use suitable level translation. Open-drain outputs need pull-ups to a compatible rail. Check the datasheet before connecting anything.
  • Use free GPIOs that can perform the intended function. Keep the UART pins free for the USB-to-UART link, and avoid the board’s boot-strapping, USB, flash, PSRAM, and on-board LED pins. The ESP configuration permits up to six PWM actuator instances and four quadrature feedback instances, subject to available pins and device limits.

When you have the parts, install the app or open the wiring page to check the connections. For the robot, start at Pi preparation and continue to the Pi UART wiring.
